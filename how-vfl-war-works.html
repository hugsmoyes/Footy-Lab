How VFL WAR works

The formula step by step · What changes from AFL WAR · 2024 to 2026 home and away seasons
VFL WAR is how many extra wins a player adds compared with a fringe VFL-listed player at his position. 1.0 WAR is about one extra win. It uses the same formula as AFL WAR. The difference is the data: Champion Data's equity numbers aren't published for the VFL, so each player's equity is estimated from the stats the VFL does publish.

1Estimated value per game

In the AFL, a player's value starts with Champion Data equity: how much his touches change his team's chance of scoring. The VFL doesn't publish it, so it's estimated.

The estimate comes from a model trained on four AFL seasons (2023 to 2026, 2,250 player seasons). It predicts each player's equity per game using only the stats the VFL also publishes. Tested on players it hadn't seen, it tracks real equity closely (correlation 0.93). Running the whole WAR formula on AFL players with the estimate in place of real equity gives numbers that match real AFL WAR at 0.90, and between 0.87 and 0.94 at every position.

Each weight is what that stat adds once all the others are already counted. Kicks, handballs, marks and uncontested possessions sit close to zero for that reason: volume on its own doesn't add much, but what a player does with the ball does. Frees for is left out. A free kick is already counted as a contested possession and a disposal, so on top of those it only pulled the estimate slightly negative, which is hard to justify and made no difference to accuracy.

Tackles are added on top at a quarter of a point each, the same as AFL WAR.

Value per game = estimated equity + 0.25 × tackles

Two AFL inputs are missing. The VFL has no time on ground, so everything is per game rather than per full game. There are no defensive one-on-one numbers either, so that part of the AFL formula is left out.

2Minus the league average

Take away the VFL average for that season, weighted by games. In 2026 that was 6.22 points per game. What's left is V: points above an average VFL player.

3Team adjustment

Each club gets an adjustment so its players add up to its actual points margin for the season, shared out by games played. That's T. It uses every home and away result from 2024 to 2026, checked against the official ladders. Players who changed clubs mid-season get each club's adjustment for the games they played there.

4Minus VFL replacement level

Replacement level is the player any VFL club can call on: VFL-listed players outside their club's top 20 for games played, pooled across 2024 to 2026. Players on AFL lists are left out of this group. They usually play fewer VFL games but are better players, and counting them would set the bar too high.

The cut-off is top 20, not the AFL's top 25, so that both models measure from the same standard. With top 20, a team of replacement players would win about 1.6 of 18 VFL games. In the AFL model, a replacement team wins 2.2 of 23. Those are the same rate.

There are no official VFL positions, so each player's position is estimated from his stats each season, using a model trained on AFL players' labelled positions (right 83% of the time on AFL players).

5 + 6Accumulate, then convert to wins

Multiply points above replacement per game by games played, then divide by 100.5, the points of margin it takes to add one win across a VFL season. That figure comes from fitting every VFL club's season margin against its wins. It's close to the AFL figure of 104.6.

Players on AFL lists are rated on their VFL games only. A player who spends half the year in the AFL finishes lower than he would have over a full VFL season.

What the numbers mean

The whole VFL adds up to about 155 to 165 WAR a season, close to the AFL's 167. In 2026 the leader was George Stevens on 1.67, three players finished on 1.5 or more, and 16 on 1.0 or more.

Limits

It's an estimate of an estimate. Equity is predicted from box-score stats, so a player whose value comes from things the box score misses (smart positioning, the quality of his kicks rather than the number) will be under or over-rated. On AFL players this costs about 10% of accuracy.

Positions are estimated. A player the model labels wrongly is measured against the wrong replacement level. You'll see the label on every player in the dashboard.

No time on ground. A player who's subbed off early gets the same game count as one who plays the full match.

The same blind spots as AFL WAR apply too: key defenders compress at the top end, and taggers grade badly because their job is to shrink an opponent's numbers.
