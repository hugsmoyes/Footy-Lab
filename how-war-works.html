How WAR works

The formula step by step · The 25 re-ranked · 2026 home and away season, finals excluded
The Formula
The 25 Re-Ranked
WAR (Wins Above Replacement) is how many extra wins a player adds compared with a fringe player at his position. 1.0 WAR is about one extra win. It's built the same way as the established WAR models in baseball and ice hockey: work out how many points of margin a player adds, compare him with a replacement-level player in his position, then turn points into wins. Play more, accumulate more.

1Raw value per full game

Everything is measured per full game, not per game played. Full games are time on ground added up, so a player who plays 80% of every match for 20 matches has 16 full games. That stops sub-heavy and injury-shortened games counting the same as a full shift.

Each player's raw value comes from three things:

Champion Data equityThe change in scoring chance every time a player touches the ball. Covers kicking, handballing, marking, winning it at stoppages and creating shots. Already in points of margin.
×1
TacklesEquity misses defensive work done without the ball, so each tackle is worth a quarter of a point. Fitted against team defence numbers.
+0.25
Defensive one-on-one lossesLosses above what's expected for how many contests a player stands in (0.274 losses per contest is the league norm). Losing more than that costs two points each.
−2.0

Raw value = (equity + 0.25 × tackles − 2.0 × extra one-on-one losses) ÷ full games

2Minus the league average

Take away the league-average raw value for that season (weighted by full games). In 2026 that was 9.57 points per full game. What's left is V: points above an average player. An average player sits on zero.

3Team adjustment

Individual stats never add up perfectly to a club's results. Some teams defend well as a unit, some win games in ways the stats don't catch. So each club gets an adjustment that makes its players' values add up to its actual margin for the season, shared out by time on ground. That's T. It's usually small, within a point or so per full game either way.

4Minus replacement level

Replacement level is the player any club can call on for free: the fringe guys outside a club's top 25 for game time. They're the ones who come in when someone's hurt. Their average value, pooled across 2023 to 2026, is the baseline for each position. That's R.

This is where position comes in. A replacement midfielder is still close to average, because there are plenty of decent mids who can't crack a best 22. A replacement key forward is a long way below average, because good ones are scarce. So the same raw number is worth more at a scarce position.

5 + 6Accumulate, then convert to wins

Multiply points above replacement per full game by full games played. Then divide by 104.6, the number of points of margin it takes to add one win across a season. That figure comes from fitting every club's season margin against its wins.

WAR is a counting stat. It doesn't adjust for small samples, so a player who misses half the year finishes lower than he would have over a full season. That's deliberate.

What the numbers mean

Across a season the whole league adds up to about 167 WAR. A team made entirely of replacement players would win about 2 of its 23 games. In 2026 the leader was Bontempelli on 2.00, seven players finished on 1.5 or more, and 37 on 1.0 or more.

What was tested and left out

Pressure acts raise a team's turnover rate, but they don't lower the opposition's equity once it has the ball, so they added nothing on top of what equity and tackles already capture. Spoils and rebound 50s were tested too. Neither improved the model, so both stayed out. Goals aren't a separate input: equity already values a shot by where it's taken from and whether it goes through.

Two known blind spots. Key defenders are compressed at the top end, because a lot of their best work is stopping the ball rather than winning it. Taggers and shutdown players grade badly, as they do in every model like this, because their job is to make an opponent's numbers smaller, not their own bigger.
